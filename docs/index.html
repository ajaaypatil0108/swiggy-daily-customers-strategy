1 · The problem: food delivery is a treat, not a habit

In FY24 (April 2023 to March 2024), Swiggy's food delivery business handled 577.7 million orders worth ₹24,717.4 crore, from an average of 12.73 million monthly transacting users.1 The average order was ₹428.2

That works out to about 1.58 million orders a day, and only 3.78 orders per user per month. Most users order less than once a week.

An average monthly user orders on about 4 of 30 days (3.78 orders a month).

The insight that shapes this plan: to add daily customers, Swiggy has to win a meal people need every day. Weekend dinners and cravings are already Swiggy's strength. The everyday meal it barely touches is the weekday lunch, and at ₹428 the average restaurant order is too expensive to buy five times a week.

2 · How Swiggy makes money

  • Restaurant commissions, charged as a share of each order, plus advertising that restaurants buy to be seen in the app.3
  • Customer fees: delivery fees and a per-order platform fee, which Swiggy introduced in April 2023.4
  • Membership: Swiggy One, which customers pay for in exchange for free deliveries and discounts.10

After delivery and other variable costs, the average food order left Swiggy a contribution of about ₹24.4 in FY24.3 That number matters later: it is the most Swiggy can spend to win one extra order without losing money on it.

What stops a daily habit: the price of a restaurant meal, delivery and platform fees on every order, uncertain delivery times at the lunch rush, and the fact that many people want simple home-style food on a weekday, not restaurant food.

4 · Competition (as of Feb 2024)

Zomato

The larger rival: ₹32,224 crore of food delivery GOV in FY24 against Swiggy's ₹24,700 crore.5 It relaunched its Zomato Gold membership in January 2023, and in February 2023 launched Zomato Everyday, home-style meals from ₹89 in Gurugram.6 Everyday is the most direct competitor to strategy 1.

Local tiffin services and dabbawalas

The real competition for weekday lunch. When Swiggy launched Daily in 2019, it described the daily meal market as highly unorganised, with tiffin services and home chefs selling through chat groups and word of mouth.7 They are cheap and trusted, but hard to discover and pay.

Dunzo

A hyperlocal delivery app that competed with Instamart and Genie, Swiggy's courier service,9 rather than with food delivery. It had been cutting back operations for months before its app and website went offline in January 2025.15

Removed: Uber Eats

The original draft analysed Uber Eats as a rival. Uber sold Uber Eats India to Zomato in January 2020,16 so it was not a competitor in 2024.

6 · Three strategies

1

Bring home-style meals into the main app

The idea. Weekday lunch and dinner from home chefs, tiffin services and small cloud kitchens, sold inside the Swiggy app as single meals or 5-, 10- and 20-meal plans at roughly ₹90–150 a meal.

What is new compared with Daily (2019). No separate app to download. It launches only in office-dense neighbourhoods where people are back at their desks, and it also targets people living away from home, not just office workers. Orders close the night before, so each kitchen cooks to a known count and one rider can carry several meals to the same office building or PG cluster.

Main risk. Hybrid work leaves offices half-empty on some days, home kitchens need food-safety checks, and Zomato Everyday is already in the market.

2

Partner with Mumbai's dabbawalas for lunch delivery

The idea. In Mumbai, dabbawalas carry Swiggy's home-style lunch orders from strategy 1 to offices. Customers order in the Swiggy app as usual; kitchens hand over tiffins at collection points by mid-morning; dabbawalas deliver them on their existing station-wise routes, and Swiggy pays a fee per tiffin.

What is new. In 2015 the dabbawalas trained Swiggy's riders. Here they become a delivery partner.

Why the timing fits. By 2026 the network had shrunk from nearly 5,000 dabbawalas before the pandemic to about 3,000, carrying just over 50,000 lunchboxes a day.13 Before the pandemic it delivered about 200,000.14 A dabbawala typically serves 15 to 20 customers.13 The network has spare capacity and needs volume; Swiggy needs a cheap way to deliver a ₹100 lunch. One dabbawala carrying many tiffins on a fixed route should cost less per meal than one rider making one trip per order.

Main risk. It only works for a fixed lunch window. There is no formal registration of dabbawalas,13 so contracting and accountability for late or lost meals need careful design.

3

A weekday "lunch streak" inside Swiggy One

The idea. Swiggy One and One Lite members who order lunch on a set number of weekdays in a month unlock perks: member-only lunch combos funded by restaurants, and priority delivery at the lunch rush. A progress bar on the home screen shows how close they are.

What is new. Swiggy One and One Lite already reward members with free deliveries, which make each order cheaper. The streak rewards how often members order, which is what builds a habit.

How to keep it profitable. Each member's target is set above their own usual pattern (for example, from 4 lunch orders a month to 8), so Swiggy does not pay for orders that would have happened anyway. Rewards are mostly restaurant-funded or non-cash, because each extra order is worth only about ₹24.4 to Swiggy.3

Main risk. Members learn to wait for perks, and a frequency program is easy for Zomato to copy.

Ideas from the original draft that were set aside: a healthy-food section, niche diets, virtual kitchen brands, app improvements and eco-friendly packaging are all reasonable, but they mainly improve choice and experience rather than how often people order. Hyperlocal delivery and subscriptions were also proposed, but Swiggy already had Instamart, Genie and Swiggy One.

Reading the base case: together the three strategies add about 96,625 orders on an average day, a 6.1% increase. Home-style meals do most of the work; the dabbawala partnership is small in volume because it is limited to Mumbai's lunch hour, but it is what could make a ₹100 lunch cheap enough to deliver there.

Why this replaces the original percentages: the Feb 2024 draft claimed each strategy could add 5–20% more daily users, which together implied 30–55% growth, with no basis given. Built on Swiggy's own FY24 figures and the stated assumptions, the estimate ranges from +1.7% to +14.4%.

8 · The economics

Cheap meals need cheap delivery

If Swiggy's take is a share of the order, a ₹100 home-style meal earns it about 23% of what a ₹428 restaurant order does, while a one-rider, one-order delivery costs about the same. Home-style meals only make money if deliveries are batched: orders placed the night before, fixed lunch windows, many meals per trip, and in Mumbai, dabbawala routes.

Rewards have a hard ceiling

An average order contributed about ₹24.4 in FY24.3 A lunch-streak reward that costs more than that per extra order loses money, and any reward paid on an order the member would have placed anyway is pure cost. That is why targets are personal and rewards are mostly restaurant-funded.

Watch value, not just volume. More orders can still mean less revenue if a ₹100 lunch replaces a ₹428 dinner. The pilots should track gross order value and contribution per customer alongside order counts.

  • Orders per monthly user, per month (baseline 3.78).
  • Extra orders compared with the control group, not total orders, so growth that would have happened anyway is not counted.
  • Contribution per order for home-style meals, and plan renewal rate.
  • On-time rate and cost per meal in the dabbawala pilot.

10 · Risks and limits

  • Assumed numbers: adoption, frequency and cannibalisation rates are illustrative. Pilot results should replace them.
  • Hybrid work: Daily closed once because offices emptied. Launching only where offices are busy reduces this risk but does not remove it.
  • Cannibalisation: cheap lunches can replace pricier restaurant orders, raising order counts while lowering revenue.
  • Competition: Zomato Everyday was already live, and any frequency program can be copied. The lasting advantage has to come from kitchen supply and low delivery cost.
  • Timing of the data: the FY24 baseline comes from figures Swiggy published for its 2024 IPO, after this project's Feb 2024 date. It is used because it is the closest full year.
